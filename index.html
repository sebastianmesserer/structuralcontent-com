Overview

Put content to work across the whole business.

Most content teams work from requests, campaign calendars, and briefs. That can leave valuable business signals untouched – even when content could help solve the problem.

Structural Content helps marketing and content teams decide what content to create to support their business goals – across the entire company. It analyzes company documentation and connected systems, such as CRMs, project-management tools, support queues, and product data, to find where content can solve real operational needs.

It then turns those opportunities into actionable campaign briefs for the content team, with the deeper business context. Instead of adding more requests to the backlog, Structural Content helps teams focus their effort on content that can support a specific strategic priority. That lets content teams support the business wherever they are needed, beyond just lead generation and direct product marketing.

How it works

From business signal to actionable campaign brief.

1

Understand the business priority

Structural Content reads strategic priorities from planning documents, goal systems, and other company context.

2

Analyze the signals around it

It analyzes connected systems – including CRM, project-management, support, product, and communication tools – to find where customers, prospects, or internal teams have real operational needs that content can solve.

3

Identify high-impact content opportunities

It determines where content can solve a real operational need and move the priority: for example, by supporting retention, adoption, onboarding, sales, support, or hiring.

4

Prepare the work for the content team

Each opportunity becomes an actionable campaign brief, tied to its business context and ready for production.

Examples

Content opportunities hiding in your business data.

The opportunities Structural Content finds depend on your company’s priorities and operating context. Here are two examples:

Recover revenue when customers misunderstand your product.

Structural Content can detect in CRM data that customers left because they believed a feature was missing. When that feature becomes available, it can brief the outreach campaign needed to bring those customers back – helping support retention and recover ARR.

Reduce support demand by improving onboarding content.

It can spot a cluster of support tickets around a recurring onboarding issue, then brief a campaign to improve the relevant help, education, or in-product content – helping customers succeed sooner, reduce avoidable support demand, and protect NRR.

Why it differs

It helps you decide what content to create.

Most AI content tools help teams create content faster once someone has decided what to make. Structural Content helps teams make that decision in the first place.

It connects business priorities with the signals in your company’s documentation and systems, then identifies content opportunities that are specific to the work the business needs to do now. Because every brief arrives with clear context, the content team knows what to make and why before it starts.

The unit of value is not a single generation use case, but a standing system that keeps finding the right content and structuring it into actionable campaign briefs as the business keeps moving.

Who it fits

For B2B teams that need content to support the business.

Structural Content is often a strong fit for B2B software companies around series A–B – roughly 50–200 people – especially SaaS, with a standing content team, written priorities, and at least one nameable metric under pressure. It works best when relevant teams already operate in reachable systems such as CRM, Slack, Asana, or Jira. It still works when priorities live in messy docs, slides, or spreadsheets rather than a tidy OKR tool. If your team is mainly reacting to requests while trying to prove business impact, this is the setting it is designed for.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How does Structural Content get installed in my company?

Structural Content is installed as a single-tenant system for your company. It reads where your goals live and connects to the systems of the teams owning the priority being worked, such as CRM, support, product usage, Slack, or project management tools.

The first step is not a full installation. It is a demo call, followed by a business case for your company and a pilot that validates it before full deployment.

What exactly does the system produce?

Structural Content identifies high-impact content opportunities and turns them into actionable campaign briefs, ready for production.

Every brief is tied back to a strategic priority and carries its lineage: the metric under pressure, the work it supports, and the context your content team requires.

Does Structural Content also generate the content?

No. It determines what content is worth creating and delivers a campaign brief so rich in context that production becomes the straightforward part – whether your team writes it, an agency does, or you hand the brief to an AI model. Drop a brief into the LLM of your choice and expect a strong piece back, because everything it needs to know is already in the brief.

Does this create more work for my content team?

No. Structural Content briefs are designed to replace vague content requests with clearer, higher-value work tied to real business priorities. They do not add another layer of requests; they improve the quality of what enters the backlog.

Because each brief is highly context-rich, production – by your team, an agency, or an AI model – takes less effort, not more.

Can’t we just do this with Claude, or build it ourselves?

A general AI model can help with one-off content planning. Structural Content is designed to continuously identify company-specific content opportunities from your priorities, documentation, and connected systems – and turn them into actionable work for the content team.

So the real build-vs-buy question is not whether you can access a model. It is whether you can build and maintain a high-quality system around it, and encode enough specialized content strategy expertise into that system for the outputs to be genuinely valuable. Without that, most teams end up with generic outputs rather than the specific, high-leverage content work that actually moves a strategic priority.

Is this mainly for marketing content?

No. Structural Content is built for marketing and content teams, but it can identify opportunities wherever content can support a business priority – from acquisition and retention to onboarding, adoption, support, enablement, and hiring.

It treats content as any communication that helps move an audience from a current state to a required end-state in service of a job, a metric, and a strategic priority.

How do I know my data is secure?

Structural Content runs as a dedicated instance for your company, under an Article 28 data processing agreement that commits processing to the EU/EEA and names the hosting provider, the regions, the sub-processors, and the retention and deletion terms. It reads your systems through scoped, revocable credentials; personal identifiers are filtered at the source, and analysis stays at team level. How do I know whether this is worth exploring for my company?

Structural Content is worth exploring when at least one strategic priority is clear, a metric under pressure can be named, the owning teams work in digitally reachable systems, and someone in-house owns the content queue.

It tends to be strongest where useful signals are visible across several functions, but content still reaches the business mostly through campaign planning and ad hoc requests.
