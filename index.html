Fair questions

Isn't this just an attribution tool?

No. Attribution looks back at which content worked. Structural Content looks ahead: it finds what's stopping your buyers, tells you what to make about it, and then checks whether it moved your number.

Does this mean I stop doing what I do now?

No. Same channels, same team, same tools. What changes is where your money goes. You keep the search terms and campaigns that bring in customers, add the ones only your buyers use, and stop spending time and money competing for terms that only bring in engagement that doesn't convert.

Couldn't we build this ourselves? Isn't it just Gong with an LLM?

You could try: drop your sales calls into an LLM and ask what's stopping your buyers. You'll get answers that sound good. Getting campaign briefs your team would use as they are is the hard part: connecting everything reliably, deep content strategy and AI engineering working together, months of work, and upkeep after that. Not convinced? The free audit lets you compare what Structural Content finds with what you could build yourself.

Isn't this just an SEO tool?

SEO tools start from what everyone searches for, so you get the same list as your competitors. Structural Content starts from what's stopping your buyers, which only your data knows, then finds the searches that reach them.

Is it an AI writing tool?

No, on purpose: there are plenty of good ones already. Structural Content gives you unusually rich briefs, so whoever makes your content, your AI tools, your agency, your team or a mix, knows exactly what to make and can make it fast.

Isn't this an intent-data or ABM tool?

Those start from outside signals about who might be in the market. Structural Content starts from your own customers, finds who to win and why they'd buy, and can then use outside signals to find more like them.

Isn't this stepping on sales' or CS's toes?

No. Sales and CS work one-to-one, especially with your biggest accounts. Marketing reaches whole groups at once: before the sale, and after it in lifecycle marketing, from onboarding to cross-sell. Structural Content does the group part, using what sales and CS already know. Say your CS notes show that customers who back up the whole company in their first week stay three times longer. It can suggest a campaign that brings in more companies like them, and an onboarding series that gets every new customer there in their first week.

Is it more work for my team?

Less than today. Deciding what to make usually takes research, workshops and debate, or briefing an agency and reviewing what comes back. With Structural Content, you connect your data once, and campaigns arrive in your content board as tickets, already briefed and ranked. Your team reviews them and makes the pieces, as it does now.
